# Heat & Clean Glass Oven — Design for Manufacture & Cost-Down Guide

**Company:** Nice Dreamz
**Purpose:** Help you quote the lowest, cleanest price by showing exactly where we are flexible.

---

## Please read first — how we want to work

- We assemble the finished units ourselves in the USA. You only need to supply the **separate parts**, not an assembled product.
- We strongly prefer: **flat laser-cut pieces + simple brake bends + parts that BOLT or SCREW together** — instead of welded assemblies.
- Why: hand welding, then grinding and re-polishing the welds, is the most expensive labor. Bolts and screws are cheap, fast, and consistent on machines.
- **We are happy to split any part into several simpler pieces** that bolt together if that lowers the machining cost. We do not mind a lot of screws and bolts.
- **Hidden fasteners are completely fine.** Only the visible outer surfaces must look clean and sharp — this product must look like a finished kitchen appliance.
- **Being able to rebuild the unit is a KEY requirement for us.** The heating coil, thermocouple, and ceramic sit inside and may need service over the product's life. A bolt-together construction lets us open the unit, replace an internal part, and rebuild it. Welded-shut assemblies make that impossible. This is a major reason we prefer screws over welds.
- Please use your **stock materials** wherever possible (stock tube, stock pre-perforated sheet). Avoid custom tooling unless it is truly necessary.

---

## Part-by-part cost-down suggestions

### Base Body (chamber: inner wall + outer perforated mesh + support ring + standoff fins)
This is the most expensive part, so it is where the biggest savings are.
- Instead of rolling and seam-welding two cylinders, use **stock 304 tube** for the inner wall and **stock pre-perforated 304 tube** for the outer mesh, both simply cut to length. This removes the rolling, the perforating, and the seam welds.
- **Remove the 12 standoff fins entirely.** Replace them with **two laser-cut end rings** (top and bottom) that locate both tubes concentrically and hold the 4 mm air gap. **Screw** the tubes to the rings instead of welding fins.
- This part is fully hidden inside, so a bolted construction is perfect.

### Bottom Cap
- Already very simple: one laser-cut disk, one bent lip, integral tabs. No change needed.

### Top Cap
- Make the flat ring as one laser-cut piece, and make the 4 U-channels as **4 separate bent pieces that screw to the ring from underneath** (M3 or M4), instead of welding them. No welds means no grinding and no re-polishing, and the top face stays clean.
- The channels sit down inside the slot area, hidden, so bolt-on brackets are fine there.

### Lid Assembly
- Split the welded sub-parts into **flat laser-cut pieces that screw together.**
- **Bolt the handle on** through the top (2 screws) instead of welding posts.
- **Bolt the hinge plate on** instead of welding it.
- Use **stock pre-perforated sheet** for the lid's mesh ring. Combine smaller rings into one piece where the geometry allows.

### Steel Tray
- Already simple: laser-cut 3 mm plate + 4 brake bends. No change needed. If the 3 mm bends are difficult, the lips can instead be separate strips that bolt on.

### Hinge Pin
- Just a 5 mm rod cut to 25 mm and deburred.
